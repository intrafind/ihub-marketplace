# Campaign Blueprint: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: Add-on launch to existing customers

**Request**

> Campaign brief for launching our Predictive Maintenance add-on to existing customers, 11 Jan to 26 Mar 2027.
> Goal: grow add-on revenue. Budget EUR 18,000. From HubSpot: 14 add-on demo requests last quarter; 310 maintenance
> managers and 140 plant managers at customer accounts. Last customer webinar: 120 registered, 70 attended, 21 demo
> requests. Team: 2 marketers, 5 h/week each for campaign work. Owner: Elena Varga.

**How the skill handles it**

- Stage 1: "grow add-on revenue" is not something marketing alone moves within the window, so the primary metric
  becomes demo requests, with the baseline of 14 per quarter from HubSpot.
- Stage 2: two segments, sized from CRM data; awareness level and channels come from the user's data or are marked
  [Data needed], never assumed.
- Stage 4: capacity check: 7 assets × 10 h = 70 h against 2 × 5 h × 8 weeks = 80 h of preparation time, so it fits.
- Stage 6: only the webinar has a track record; LinkedIn is a new channel with a test budget and low confidence.
  6,000 + 7,000 + 3,000 + 2,000 = 18,000, with an 11% reserve.

**Answer**

## Example 2: Vague goal, no data

**Request**

> We need a campaign to make us better known in the DACH market next year. Which channels should we use and how
> should we split the budget? Give me the best-practice split.

**How the skill handles it**

- Stage 1: "better known" fails the measurable, baselined and singular tests, so the objective is fixed first.
- Ground rule: no channel mix or budget split is presented as best practice, and no benchmarks are invented.
- Stages 2 and 6 need audience and budget data, so the skill asks for them and shows the objective it would propose.

**Answer**

There is no best-practice split I can honestly give you: the right mix depends on your audience, budget and past
results, and I don't have those yet. Let's make the objective measurable first, because channels and budget follow
from it.

**A draft objective to react to** [AI suggestion]
- Business goal: grow pipeline in Germany, Austria and Switzerland
- Primary metric: one of aided brand awareness in a target-group survey, branded search volume in DACH, or
  inbound leads from DACH. Which one can you measure today?
- Target and timeline: [Data needed]
- Baseline: [Data needed]

**What I need for the rest of the brief**
1. Who should know you: industries, company sizes and job titles
2. Your total budget for the campaign, or a ceiling
3. Past results by channel in DACH (cost per lead, engagement), from your analytics or ad platforms
4. Team capacity: people and hours per week for content production

With these I'll build the segments, choose channels on your own data, and allocate the budget with a reserve and
reallocation triggers. [Framework methodology]
