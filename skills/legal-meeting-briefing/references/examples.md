# Legal Meeting Briefing: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: Call with a supplier's counsel on a late-delivery penalty

**Request**

> Prep me for a call on Thu 19 Nov 2026 with Dr. Jan Hollmann, counsel for our supplier Brenner Hydraulik GmbH.
> For us: me (Katrin Vogt, Head of Legal), Moritz Lenz (Purchasing). PO-88120, EUR 640,000, delivered 6 weeks
> late. Supply agreement §9: penalty 0.5% of order value per started week, cap 5%. We claimed EUR 19,200 on 3 Nov.
> Brenner calls a strike at their sub-supplier force majeure; §14 excludes such strikes. I may settle down to
> EUR 12,000; lower needs the CFO. Don't mention our internal downtime-cost estimate.

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: people outside the organization, so a **client / counterparty call**, Playbook C, moderate prep.
- Talking points rest only on the facts given (6 × 0.5% × 640,000 = 19,200, within the 5% cap).
- Authority limits come from the user; the downtime estimate goes into "off-limits".
- Marks privilege; adds the action log.

**Answer**

> Not legal advice: a draft for review by a qualified legal professional. Privileged and confidential; internal
> distribution to Katrin Vogt and Moritz Lenz only.

```
EXTERNAL CALL PREP
==================
Counterparty:           Dr. Jan Hollmann, external counsel, Brenner Hydraulik GmbH
Attending for us:       Katrin Vogt (Head of Legal), Moritz Lenz (Purchasing)
Topic:                  Late-delivery penalty on PO-88120
What we want:           Payment of the EUR 19,200 penalty, or a settlement of at least EUR 12,000
What they probably want: Waiver of the penalty on force-majeure grounds

TALKING POINTS (most important first):
  1. Penalty is contractual — say: §9 sets 0.5% per started week, capped at 5%; six weeks gives EUR 19,200 —
     backed by: supply agreement §9, PO-88120 (EUR 640,000), delivery 6 weeks late, our claim of 3 Nov 2026
  2. Strike is not force majeure — say: §14 expressly excludes strikes at sub-suppliers — backed by: §14
  3. We value the relationship — say: we are open to closing this quickly on a reasonable basis — backed by:
     not provided — to be confirmed before the meeting (e.g. volume or duration of the relationship)

OFF-LIMITS — DO NOT:
  - Mention our internal estimate of downtime costs
  - Offer any figure below EUR 12,000
  - Agree to changes to §9 or §14 for future orders

AUTHORITY LIMITS:
  Katrin Vogt may agree a settlement of EUR 12,000 or more.
  Anything below EUR 12,000 needs CFO approval after the call.

LIKELY QUESTIONS:
  Q: Wasn't the strike outside Brenner's control?
  A: §14 places strikes at sub-suppliers within Brenner's risk; the contract settles this point.

  Q: Will you waive it for a discount on the next order?
  A: We'll take any proposal back internally.

FALLBACKS:
  If they press on force majeure: refer to §14 and ask on which clause they rely instead.
  If they raise our own delays or quality issues: note it, say we'll respond in writing.

AFTER THE CALL:
  - Put any agreement in writing within 2 business days
  - Add the call notes to the matter file
  - Flag any new commitments or shifted positions to the CFO
```

```

====================================
| No. | What will be done | Who | Due by | State | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Send action log to attendees | Katrin Vogt | 20 Nov 2026 | open | Within 24 hours |
| 2 | Confirm outcome in writing to Dr. Hollmann | Katrin Vogt | 23 Nov 2026 | open | 2 business days after the call |
| 3 | Seek CFO approval if offer < EUR 12,000 | Katrin Vogt | [date] | open | Only if needed |
```

## Example 2: Meeting with a market surveillance authority, gaps in the facts

**Request**

> The state market surveillance authority meets us on 26 Nov 2026 about overheating reports for our FlexHeat 2000
> fan heater, after two consumer reports. For us: CEO Ruth Albers, Head of Quality Tim Kranz, me (Sven Ott, legal).
> Units affected: unknown, quality still testing. Write the prep. Can we promise a voluntary recall by December?

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: a meeting with a regulator, so Playbook E, high prep.
- Unknown unit numbers and test results are marked "not provided — to be confirmed before the meeting".
- No authority limits given; the recall promise is an unvalidated timeline commitment, so it becomes an
  authority item to decide before the meeting.

**Answer**

> Not legal advice: a draft for review by a qualified legal professional. Privileged and confidential.

On the recall: don't offer it unless management has decided it and the date is checked internally; an unvalidated
timeline commitment is ruled out below. If you want it on the table, decide before 26 November.

```
REGULATORY MEETING PREP
=======================
Authority:          State market surveillance authority, [department: not provided — to be confirmed before the meeting]
Attending for them: not provided — to be confirmed before the meeting
Attending for us:   Ruth Albers (CEO), Tim Kranz (Head of Quality), Sven Ott (Legal)
Topic:              Overheating reports concerning the FlexHeat 2000 fan heater
Background:         Inquiry; the authority made contact after two consumer reports

MESSAGES:
  Core message:     We take the reports seriously and are investigating them systematically.
  Supporting points:
    1. We know of the two consumer reports the authority refers to.
    2. Quality testing of the FlexHeat 2000 is under way; results expected [not provided — to be confirmed
       before the meeting].
    3. We will share confirmed results with the authority as they become available.

MESSAGING DISCIPLINE — DO NOT:
  - Speculate about the number of affected units or the cause before testing is complete
  - Offer information beyond the FlexHeat 2000 and the two reports
  - Commit to a recall or any timeline without internal validation
  - Comment on competitors or on how the industry generally operates
  - Concede any defect or accept liability unless counsel has advised it

DOCUMENTS:
  - Take along: the two consumer reports
  - Leave behind: internal test drafts, internal emails on the reports, this prep

AUTHORITY LIMITS:
  Not provided — to be confirmed before the meeting. Needed:
  - What attendees may agree to (e.g. a date for submitting test results)
  - Whether a voluntary recall may be offered, and who must approve it
  - Whether external counsel should be involved before or in the meeting
```

**Open preparation items before 26 November**
1. Number of units sold and in the field — Tim Kranz.
2. Status and expected date of the test results — Tim Kranz.
3. Authority limits and the recall decision — Ruth Albers.
4. The authority's attendees — Sven Ott.
