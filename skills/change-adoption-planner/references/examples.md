## Example 1: Replacing Excel pipeline lists with a CRM

**Request**

> We're moving our 45 field sales reps (regions North, South, West) from Excel pipeline lists to a CRM. Go-live is
> 1 March, with two weeks of buffer before the quarterly forecast. Sponsor: Petra Lindqvist, Sales Director. Change
> lead: Jonas Brehm (Sales Ops). Marco Feld, head of Region South (16 reps), says reps will "type instead of sell".
> The 8-person inside sales team asked for the CRM. Reps learn by doing; South is on the road four days a week.

**How the skill handles it**

- Sizing: medium on people, process, technology, skills and timeline; low on structure. Medium on two or more
  dimensions makes it Transitional, so all phases are covered at moderate depth.
- Phase 1 uses only the stakeholders the user named; no one is invented.
- Phase 2 reads Marco Feld's objection as willingness (workload), not capability.
- Phase 5 leaves targets open: no benchmark adoption rates.

## Where the resistance comes from and how to answer it
"Type instead of sell" is a willingness concern about workload, not a capability gap. Response: measure the
entry time for one visit report on the mobile app together with South reps, cut mandatory fields to what the
forecast needs, and show the result. [Framework methodology]

## Example 2: Adoption has stalled, with a benchmark question

**Request**

> Our new ticketing process went live 10 weeks ago for the 60-person IT service desk. Logins look fine: 57 of 60
> agents use the tool every week. But reopened tickets went from 41 in week 2 to 88 in week 10 and SLA breaches are
> up. Is that normal? What adoption rate should we expect?

**How the skill handles it**

- Ground rules: gives no "normal" adoption or reopen rate; compares only the user's own numbers.
- Phase 5 pattern table: adoption high, proficiency falling, which points to a training gap.
- Asks for what the diagnosis needs and does not invent who the affected agents are.

**Answer**

There is no reliable "normal" adoption rate to compare against: it depends too much on the organization. What your
own numbers show is clearer:

Reading: **adoption high, proficiency low.** People use the new process but not correctly, which points to a

1. Which ticket categories or teams account for the reopens?

3. Are there job aids at the desk today?
