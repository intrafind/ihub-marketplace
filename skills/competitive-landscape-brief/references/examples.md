# Competitive Landscape Brief: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: Brief after a competitor launch

**Request**

> Rostar just launched AI auto-scheduling. Brief for the product team, please. We're Plannity (shift planning for
> retail, DACH). What we have: Rostar press release, 3 Nov 2026: "AI auto-scheduling now available to all customers".
> Our sales notes: two deals lost in October, both prospects saw Rostar's auto-scheduling demo. Rostar pricing page
> (checked 10 Nov): per employee per month, free tier up to 15 employees. G2 review summary from our PMM: several
> 2026 reviews say Rostar has no works-council approval workflow. LinkedIn: Rostar hiring 4 account executives for
> Spain and Italy. Our docs: rule-based scheduling (no AI), works-council approval workflow, DATEV payroll export.

**How the skill handles it**

- Phase 1 sorts the material by source type and lists unknowns as research tasks.
- Phase 2 rates shipped capabilities only, each with a source; Rostar's DATEV export stays Unknown.
- Phase 3: auto-scheduling is an Active threat (field evidence from lost deals); the works-council gap is a
  Differentiation opportunity; the southern Europe hiring is not in Plannity's stronghold, so it is a signal only.
- Phase 4: capacity is unknown, so Build is left for planning.

**Answer**

# Competitor intelligence brief: Rostar
Date: 17 Nov 2026    |    Author: Product Strategy    |    Classification: Internal

## 1. Key takeaways
Rostar now ships AI auto-scheduling, which cost us two deals in October. It still appears to lack a
works-council approval workflow, which matters to our DACH retail customers. [AI assessment — verify]

## 2. What we know, and from where
```
INTELLIGENCE INVENTORY — Rostar
  Known (sourced):    Auto-scheduling GA (press release, 3 Nov 2026); demoed in 2 lost deals (sales notes,
                      Oct 2026); per-employee pricing with free tier to 15 employees (pricing page, 10 Nov 2026);
                      4 AE openings in Spain and Italy (LinkedIn, Nov 2026)
  Partially known:    No works-council workflow (G2 reviews 2026, secondhand summary; not checked in product)
  Unknown:            Auto-scheduling quality and constraints; price per employee; DATEV export
  Last updated:       17 Nov 2026
```

## 3. Capabilities side by side
```

| Dimension             | Plannity                    | Rostar                             | Notes                       |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Automatic scheduling  | Adequate (rule-based; docs) | Strong (field: 2 demos, Oct 2026)  | Their edge in lost deals    |
| Works-council approval| Strong (our docs)           | Weak (G2 reviews, 2026)            | Verify in a trial           |
| Payroll export (DATEV)| Strong (our docs)           | Unknown                            | Research task               |
| Entry pricing         | Your model                  | Free to 15 employees (pricing page)| Affects small stores        |
```
[From user/knowledge source]

## 4. How they position themselves
- Value proposition: AI scheduling that saves store managers time. [From user/knowledge source]
- Go-to-market: free tier suggests self-serve entry; new AEs signal sales-led growth in Spain and Italy.
- Pricing: per employee per month, free tier up to 15 employees.
- Differentiation claim: AI scheduling; backed by the two field demos, quality unverified.

## 5. Threats and openings for us
- **Threat — Active:** auto-scheduling. Customers weigh it, Rostar is strong, we are adequate. [Analysis framework]
- **Opportunity — Differentiation:** works-council approval, required for many DACH retailers with a works council.
- **Market movement:** southern Europe hiring signals expansion outside DACH; not our stronghold today, watch only.

## 6. How we should respond
```
RESPONSE RECOMMENDATION:
  Trigger:            Rostar AI auto-scheduling GA, 3 Nov 2026
  Classification:     Threat — Active
  Impact:             New-business win rate in DACH retail; product positioning
  Response options:
    1. [Build]      — Constraint-based schedule suggestions; scope and timeline need engineering sizing
    2. [Partner]    — Integrate a third-party optimization engine
    3. [Reposition] — Lead with "compliant schedules": works-council approval and labor rules built in
    4. [Monitor]    — Track deals where auto-scheduling is raised
  Recommended option: 3 now, because it uses an existing strength and needs no build capacity; take option 1 to
                      Q1 planning once capacity is known.
  Decision owner:     Head of Product
  Urgency:            This quarter
```
[AI assessment — verify]

## 7. Still to find out
- Auto-scheduling quality: run a Rostar free-tier trial (Product, 2 weeks).
- Works-council gap: confirm in the trial, not only from reviews.
- DATEV export and price per employee: check documentation and the pricing page.

## 8. When to revisit
After the trial, at Q1 planning, and after any further loss to Rostar.

## Example 2: Request without competitor data (German)

**Request**

> Wir brauchen bis Freitag einen Wettbewerbsbericht zu Taskwerk für die Strategieklausur. Wie ist deren
> Marktanteil, und was ist bei denen preislich üblich?

**How the skill handles it**

- No competitor data supplied; market share and pricing never come from training data.
- Ground rules: returns the empty template with the data it needs, and offers a web search.
- Answers in German.

**Answer**

Zu Taskwerk liegen mir keine Quellen vor; Marktanteil oder Preise aus dem Gedächtnis wären nicht belegbar.
Diesen Rahmen fülle ich mit Ihren Daten:


**Was ich brauche:**
1. Ihre eigenen Funktionskategorien, damit der Vergleich in Ihrer Sprache bewertet wird.
2. Primärquellen: Produktdokumentation, Testzugang oder Demo-Notizen zu Taskwerk.
3. Feldwissen: Vertriebsnotizen, verlorene oder gewonnene Deals gegen Taskwerk, Kundeneinwände.
4. Preise: deren Preisseite oder Angebote, die Ihnen vorliegen.
5. Marktanteil: nur aus einem Analystenbericht oder einer Studie, die Sie haben. Ohne Quelle bleibt er „unbekannt“.
