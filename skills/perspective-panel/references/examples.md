# Perspective Panel: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: Stress-testing a plan to drop the free tier

**Request**

> We're a 180-person SaaS company (project planning tool). Leadership wants to drop our free tier on 1 April and
> move everyone to a 14-day trial. Facts: 2,400 active free accounts; 144 of them converted to paid last year (6%);
> our 8-person support team spends about 30% of its time on free users. I present this to the management board
> next week. Give me the viewpoints I should expect.

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: the subject, the decision (board presentation) and the numbers are all given, so no clarifying question.
- "When to use which panel": a business decision, so Customer, Finance and Sales, plus Skeptic as the required
  critical voice. Four viewpoints; Operations is left out because support cost is already covered by Finance.
- Step 3: concerns use only the user's figures (144 conversions, 2.4 FTE of support time); a missing figure (revenue
  per converted account) becomes a question, not an invented number.
- Step 5: no winner picked, because the user did not ask for a recommendation.

**Answer**

**What we're examining:** dropping the free tier on 1 April in favour of a 14-day trial, to be presented to the
management board next week. Context: 2,400 free accounts, 144 conversions last year (6%), about 30% of an 8-person
support team (2.4 FTE) spent on free users. `[From your material]`

**Panel**
1. Existing free customer: cares most about keeping a tool their team already depends on
2. Finance: cares most about cost to serve and predictable revenue
3. Sales: cares most about pipeline volume and lead quality
4. Skeptic: cares most about what the plan assumes without evidence

### Existing free customer
Overall: opposed: the change takes something away with little notice and no clear path.

What looks good from here
- A trial with full features could show me paid capabilities I never tried. `[Viewpoint]`

What worries me
- My projects live in your tool; 1 April gives me weeks, not months, to decide or migrate.
- If I leave, I take my recommendation with me: free users often bring the tool into a paying employer.

What I'd ask before agreeing
- What happens to my data on 1 April, and how long can I export it?

What would change my mind
- A discounted first year or a read-only mode for existing free accounts.

### Finance
Overall: supports with conditions: the cost saving is real, the revenue effect is unknown.

What looks good from here
- 2.4 FTE of support capacity freed up, or not hired for as the customer base grows. `[From your material]`

What worries me
- The 144 conversions from free are revenue we could lose; the plan does not say what they are worth.
- Hosting cost for free accounts is not in the material, so the saving may be over- or understated.

What I'd ask before agreeing
- What annual revenue did last year's 144 converted accounts bring in, and what share of all new revenue is that?

What would change my mind
- A conversion estimate for trials, backed by a pilot rather than a hope.

### Sales
Overall: skeptical: the free tier is our cheapest lead source.

What looks good from here
- Trial users have a deadline, which creates a natural moment for a sales call.

What worries me
- Fewer sign-ups at the top of the funnel; a 14-day trial is short for a planning tool that needs a real project to
  prove itself.

What I'd ask before agreeing
- How many of today's paying customers started on the free tier?

What would change my mind
- Evidence from a pilot that trial sign-ups convert at least as well as free accounts do today.

### Skeptic
Overall: skeptical: the plan treats the 6% as the free tier's only value.

What looks good from here
- Forcing a clear decision on what the free tier is for is overdue.

What worries me
- The 6% counts direct upgrades only; referrals and word of mouth from free users are not measured at all.
- Changing the offer and the date at once leaves no way to tell which effect caused what.

What I'd ask before agreeing
- What would make us reverse the decision, and how would we see it within one quarter?

What would change my mind
- A staged rollout: trial for new sign-ups first, existing free accounts later.

## Where the panel agrees
- The freed support capacity is real; the open question is the revenue side, not the cost side.
- Existing free accounts need a longer runway than 1 April.

## Where the panel clashes
| Issue | Side A (who) | Side B (who) | What the clash is really about |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | Cut on 1 April (Finance) | Stage it (Customer, Skeptic) | Speed of saving vs. risk of churn and backlash |
| Value of free users | Only the 6% counts (Finance) | They also bring referrals (Sales, Skeptic) | What we measure vs. what we can't measure yet |

## Blind spots
- Legal and contractual effects of ending a free plan (terms of service, data deletion under GDPR): ask Legal.

## Takeaways
1. The biggest open question is the revenue behind the 144 conversions; get that number before the board meeting.
2. Splitting the change (trial for new sign-ups from 1 April, existing free accounts later with an export window)
   answers the Customer and Skeptic concerns.
3. Be ready for: "How many of our paying customers started free?"

## Suggested next step
Ask Finance and Sales Ops for two numbers this week: revenue from last year's 144 conversions, and the share of
current paying customers whose first account was free.

## Example 2: Vague subject in German

**Request**

> Kannst du unsere Strategie mal aus verschiedenen Blickwinkeln kritisch beleuchten? Ich muss nächste Woche dazu
> etwas sagen.

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: "unsere Strategie" is the case the skill names as too vague to critique; there is no plan, decision or
  material to examine.
- The skill asks exactly one clarifying question and does not invent a strategy to criticise.
- It answers in German because the request is German, and says which panel it would use once the subject is clear.

**Answer**

Gern. Damit die Blickwinkel wirklich etwas Brauchbares sagen, brauche ich zuerst den konkreten Gegenstand:

**Um welche Entscheidung oder welchen Teil der Strategie geht es, und was haben Sie dazu schon schriftlich?**
(zum Beispiel: „Eintritt in den französischen Markt 2027", mit dem Strategiepapier oder den Folien als Upload)

Sobald ich das habe, stelle ich ein Panel aus vier Sichtweisen zusammen, etwa Vorstand, Kunde, Wettbewerber und
Risikomanager als kritische Stimme, und schließe mit den Punkten, auf die Sie nächste Woche vorbereitet sein
sollten.
