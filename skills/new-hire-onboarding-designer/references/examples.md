# New Hire Onboarding Designer: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: 30/60/90 plan and buddy for a senior engineer

**Request**

> 30/60/90 plan and buddy for Jonas Albrecht, Senior Backend Engineer (P3), Payments team (6 people), manager
> Leonie Hartmann, starts Mon 4 Jan 2027. Hybrid, Hamburg, permanent, external, 5 years of Java in e-commerce. Works most with Finance Ops and SRE. First real project: moving refunds to the new
> payment provider. Buddy candidates: Tim Ruge (Payments, 2 years, volunteered), Sara Peters (our tech lead,
> 4 years), Felix Wendt (Payments, joined in October).

**How the skill handles it**

- "Start with the context": all eleven fields are present, so it writes the plan without asking.
- Adapting: technical role (codebase orientation, first PR as milestone) and senior hire (shorter learning phase, listening tour).
- Buddy criteria: Tim passes every check that can be verified; Felix has under 6 months' tenure.
- Ground rules: every item is tagged; nothing about local policies or system names is invented.

**Answer**

> Note: not employment law advice. Have HR and, where relevant, employment counsel check this plan.

```
RAMP-UP 1 of 3 · LEARN · 4 Jan through 2 Feb 2027
----------------------------------------------
Aim of this stretch: a solid grounding in the payments platform, the team and its partners.

What to learn
  1. Explain the payment flow end to end, including refunds [Based on the role details]
  2. Finish every compulsory compliance course [Standard onboarding practice]
  3. Meet Finance Ops and SRE and know what each needs from Payments [Based on the role details]

Main activities
  - Architecture walkthrough with Sara Peters, week 1 [AI proposal — HR to confirm]
  - Local dev environment set up and first PR merged by end of week 2 (milestone) [Standard onboarding practice]
  - Listening tour: 1:1s with Finance Ops and SRE leads, weeks 2–3 [Standard onboarding practice]

What should be true by the end
  - Explains, in their own words, what the team is aiming for and what it is prioritizing now
  - Every required training and compliance item is finished
  - Finds his way around the core tools and systems without help

How we judge it (reviewed in the day-30 conversation)
  - First PR merged; at least two code reviews given [AI proposal — HR to confirm]
  - Written one-page summary of the current refund flow, reviewed by Sara [AI proposal — HR to confirm]

Support in place
  - 30-minute 1:1 with Leonie every week
  - 15-minute buddy check-in every day, thinning out to whenever needed
```

```
RAMP-UP 2 of 3 · CONTRIBUTE · 3 Feb through 4 Mar 2027
---------------------------------------------------
Aim of this stretch: deliver work that counts on the refund migration.

What to contribute
  1. Design for moving refunds to the new payment provider, reviewed by the team [Based on the role details]
  2. One improvement idea from a newcomer's view, e.g. on test or release flow [Standard onboarding practice]
  3. Agree the refund reconciliation needs with Finance Ops [AI proposal — HR to confirm]

Main activities
  - Owns the refund migration design; Sara reviews
  - One week shadowing SRE on-call [AI proposal — HR to confirm]

What should be true by the end
  - At least one substantial piece of work delivered without hand-holding
  - Brings an informed point of view to planning

How we judge it (reviewed in the day-60 conversation)
  - Design accepted by the team and Finance Ops
  - First migration slice in production or ready for release

Support in place
  - Weekly 1:1 with Leonie; buddy on request
```

```
RAMP-UP 3 of 3 · OWN · 5 Mar through 3 Apr 2027
--------------------------------------------
Aim of this stretch: fully productive as a P3, owning refunds.

What to own
  1. Run the refund migration to completion; Leonie steps in only on escalations [Based on the role details]
  2. Propose one technical initiative for next quarter [Standard onboarding practice]
  3. Take part in planning and review rituals as a full member [Standard onboarding practice]

What should be true by the end
  - Works independently at the level expected for a P3
  - Agreed development plan for the next 6–12 months (hand off to development-plan-designer)

How we judge it (reviewed at the day-90 review, Fri 2 Apr 2027)
  - Refund migration live, or on a plan Leonie has agreed
  - Leonie and Jonas agree what is going well, what needs developing, and next quarter's goals
```

## Example 2: German request with the context missing

**Request**

> Mach mir bitte einen Einarbeitungsplan für unsere neue Kollegin im Vertrieb, sie fängt nächsten Monat an.

**How the skill handles it**

- "Start with the context": no onboarding content is written until the details are there, so the answer is the question list.
- It asks for the missing fields and says what the plan will then contain, without inventing content.
- German request, German answer.

**Answer**

Gern. Damit der Plan zu Ihrer Kollegin passt, brauche ich vorab diese Angaben:

1. **Name** der neuen Kollegin
2. **Position** mit Level oder Einstufung (z. B. Account Manager, Key Account, Inside Sales)
3. **Team und Abteilung** sowie **Führungskraft**
4. **Genaues Startdatum**
5. **Arbeitsort:** vor Ort, hybrid oder remote, mit Stadt und Land. Das bestimmt Ausstattung, Pflichtschulungen und den Ablauf des ersten Tages.
6. **Vertragsart:** unbefristet, befristet oder freie Mitarbeit
7. **Interner Wechsel?** Wenn ja, lasse ich die allgemeine Unternehmenseinführung weg.
8. **Mitgebrachte Erfahrung:** Branche, vergleichbare Rollen
9. **Teamgröße**
10. **Wichtigste Schnittstellen** außerhalb des Teams (z. B. Marketing, Customer Success, Technik)

Hilfreich, aber nicht zwingend: Ihre Vorlaufzeiten für IT und Zugänge, Ihre Pflichtschulungen und mögliche Buddys.

Damit erstelle ich Pre-Boarding-Checklisten, Agenda für Tag 1 und Woche 1, einen 30/60/90-Tage-Plan, eine Buddy-Empfehlung und Erfolgskennzahlen. Für den Vertrieb plane ich Produktzertifizierung, Hospitation und CRM-Schulung ein, mit dem ersten eigenen Kundenkontakt als Meilenstein.
