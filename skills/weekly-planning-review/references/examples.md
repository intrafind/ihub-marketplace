# Weekly Planning Review: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: Friday close-out with connected tools

**Request**

> Friday review please, and plan next week (23–27 Nov). Asana, Outlook calendar and Teams are connected.
> My effort guesses: budget proposal 4h, battlecard 6h, webinar prep 5h, landing page copy 4h.
>
> (From the tools: done this week: v5.2 launch blog post, partner newsletter, webinar slide review.
> "Competitor battlecard update" planned this week, not started; also carried over the two weeks before.
> "Q1 campaign budget proposal" due 18 Nov, open. Case study Brauerei Falkenstein in progress, approval requested
> from Tobias Reinhardt on 10 Nov, no reply. Landing page visuals and webinar banner both waiting on Nina Vogt
> (design) since 12 Nov. Webinar Thu 26 Nov, rehearsal Wed 25 Nov. Next week's calendar: 17h of meetings out of 40h.)

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 2: every item gets one category; the overdue budget proposal gets a decision, not a note.
- Step 3: the battlecard is on its third carry-over (a pattern), and two items wait on the same person, which is one
  blocker to raise, not two.
- Step 4: effort comes from the user, not the skill. 19h needed against 17h focus time does not fit, so it
  recommends parking the landing page copy, which is blocked on the visuals anyway. Order is a suggestion.

## Example 2: Nothing connected and no effort estimates

**Request**

> Make me a plan for next week. Open: finish the specification for the new labelling machine, review the offer from
> Kroner Maschinenbau, update the FMEA for line 3, team holiday planning. Oh, and the specification was actually
> due last week.

**How the skill handles it**

- Nothing is connected: the user's list is the only source, labelled `[Per the user]`; no calendar means focus time
  cannot be calculated, and the skill does not invent meetings.
- Ground rule: never estimate specialized work. Specification, offer review and FMEA are technical work, so it asks
  for hours instead of guessing.
- The overdue item gets a proposed decision right away; the rest of the template follows once the answers are in.

**Answer**

Happy to. Here is a first sort of your items `[Per the user]`:

- **Past due:** specification for the labelling machine (due last week). This needs a decision: agree a new date,
  or finish it first thing next week `[My suggestion]`.
- **Upcoming:** review the Kroner Maschinenbau offer, update the FMEA for line 3.
- **No fixed date?** Team holiday planning.

For a plan that actually fits your week, I need four things:

1. **Effort:** how many hours of focused work do you expect for the specification, the offer review and the FMEA?
   I don't estimate specialist tasks myself.
2. **Calendar:** how many hours next week are already booked with meetings? Without a calendar I can't work out
   your available focus time.
3. **Deadlines:** is there a date for the Kroner offer (validity period) or for the FMEA?
4. **Look back:** what did you finish this week, and is anything waiting on others (for example feedback on the
   specification)?

Once I have that, you get the full weekly plan: 3–5 priorities checked against your hours, a list of nudges to
send, and what is parked on purpose.
