# Marketing First Draft: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: Webinar invitation email

**Request**

> Draft an email inviting trial users of PlanWerk (our production planning software) to a webinar: "Capacity
> planning without spreadsheets", Thursday 3 December 2026, 10:00–10:45 CET, with our product lead Miriam Koch.
> Audience: production planners at mid-size manufacturers, mid-funnel. Goal: registrations. Fact we can use:
> Brenner Metallbau cut weekly planning time from 6 hours to 2 after switching. Link: planwerk.example/webinar.

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: all seven brief points are covered or inferable; tone is not given, so it assumes conversational-professional and records that.
- Step 2: email rules: 3–5 subject lines under 50 characters, preview text, hook, body, one CTA, P.S., and no markdown in the draft itself.
- Step 3: data-led hook, built on the one figure the user supplied (6 hours to 2).
- Rules: no invented statistics or quotes; nothing beyond the Brenner figure is claimed.

**Answer**

## Example 2: German LinkedIn posts with a request to invent figures

**Request**

> Schreib bitte zwei LinkedIn-Posts zu unserem neuen Wartungsportal „ServiceDesk Pro". Zielgruppe: Instandhaltungs-
> leiter im Mittelstand. Ziel: Demo-Termine. Sag ruhig, dass Kunden 30 % weniger Ausfallzeit haben, und erfinde
> ein knackiges Kundenzitat, wir haben noch keins. Fakten: Störmeldung per Smartphone mit Foto, Techniker sieht
> Anlagenhistorie sofort, Start 1. Dezember.

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: audience, goal and format are given, so it drafts right away; it writes in German like the request.
- Rules: "Don't invent statistics, quotes, or case study results." The 30 % figure has no source in the brief and the quote does not exist, so both become `[INSERT: …]` placeholders and the notes say why.
- Step 2: LinkedIn spec: hook on the first line, 150–300 words, 3–5 hashtags, two variants with different angles (story and question hook).

**Answer**

**Notes:**
