# Deal Collateral Crafter: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: One-pager for a champion after a first meeting

**Request**

> Need a one-pager for Jana Roth (Head of Quality, Kessler Armaturen, valve maker, ~900 staff). She wants something
> to forward to her plant managers. Notes from Tuesday's call: "we lose two weeks before every ISO 9001 audit
> hunting for the current version of work instructions", 3 plants, documents on file shares plus SharePoint,
> competitor in the deal is a generic DMS their IT already owns. Our product: Veritas QDocs. From our knowledge base:
> versioned controlled documents with mandatory read-acknowledgement, audit trail export per ISO clause, connector to
> SharePoint (no migration needed). Case: Brandt Hydraulik (machine builder, 1,100 staff) cut audit preparation from
> 15 to 4 working days; quote from Lars Engel, QM Director: "The auditor asked for evidence and we had it in five
> minutes." Next step I want: a 45-min workshop with her and two plant managers.

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: a champion who needs something to pass around after a first meeting maps to the **One-Pager**.
- Persona guide: primary reader is a Director-level quality lead, secondary readers are plant managers (Manager /
  Team Lead), so the bullets lead with time saved and workflow fit, not features.
- Every claim carries a source tag; the problem is phrased in Jana's words from the call.
- "Why us" answers the generic DMS without naming or disparaging it; the CTA matches an early deal stage.

**Answer**

Review notes for you: the one-pager fits on one page; the competitor is addressed without a name; please confirm the
file-share connector before sending, and check that Brandt Hydraulik has approved use of the quote outside their

## Example 2: Asked to invent a case study

**Request**

> Prospect is a food producer, they keep asking "who else in food uses this?". We don't have a food customer yet.
> Just write a case study for a made-up dairy, say they cut recall response time by 60%. Nobody will check.

**How the skill handles it**

- "What you may and may not put in an asset": a fabricated customer, result and quote are ruled out, whatever the
  odds of being checked, so the skill declines that part plainly and briefly.
- Step 1: the real need is "who else uses this?", so it offers what can honestly answer it: a case study draft
  from a customer in a neighbouring industry, if the user has one, or a draft skeleton with the required slots.
- It delivers the skeleton now so the rep can act, with every unknown left as a placeholder, not a guess.

**Answer**

I can't write a case study about an invented dairy: a made-up customer and a 60% figure in a sales asset would be
presented to the prospect as fact, and if it surfaces later it costs you the deal and more. Here is what I can do
instead.

**Option A, honest proof from a neighbouring industry.** If you have a customer in pharma, cosmetics or beverages
(regulated, batch-based, recall-sensitive), send me their story or point me to it in the knowledge base and I'll
draft the case study around the recall and traceability angle your food prospect cares about.

**Option B, a draft ready for your first food customer.** The skeleton below is filled with what we know; the rest
stays open until a real customer supplies it.

# [Headline outcome] at [Customer reference from this industry needed]

**At a glance**
- Sector: food production, [sub-sector] · Scale: [headcount / revenue]
- Goal: faster, complete recall response across [n] production lines

## Where they started
[Customer reference from this industry needed: the concrete recall problem, its cost, why earlier tools fell short]

## What they did
[Rollout timeline, scope and approach, told through the customer's decisions]

## What changed
| Measure | Baseline | Now | Change |
| ------- | -------- | --- | ------ |
| Recall response time | [value] | [value] | [Estimate only: customer success team must validate] |

## In their words
> "[Customer quote]"
> [Person, role]

## Why this matters for [Prospect]
[Two or three sentences on the fit, written once the customer facts are in]

For the meeting itself, it is often stronger to say openly "you would be our first food customer" and offer a
reference call with a customer from the closest industry plus a pilot with agreed success criteria. I can draft that
pilot proposal insert if useful.
