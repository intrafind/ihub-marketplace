# Call Recap & Follow-Up: worked examples

Two complete examples of this skill's work. The names, companies and figures are invented: keep the structure and
the way of working, never reuse the facts.

## Example 1: Demo call from a transcript

**Request**

> Recap and follow-up email please. Demo with Weidner Haustechnik today, 17 Nov 2026, 45 min. Attendees: Carla Weidner
> (COO), Patrick Om (IT lead); me (Lukas Brandl, AE), Ana Ruiz (solutions engineer). Excerpts:
> - Carla: "Our 120 technicians get their jobs on paper. Re-planning when someone calls in sick takes my
>   dispatchers half the morning."
> - After the rescheduling demo, Carla: "That's exactly the sick-day scenario."
> - Patrick: "We use Microsoft Entra ID. Without SSO this is a non-starter."
> - Ana: "We support Entra ID SSO; I'll send the setup guide."
> - Carla: "Your price per technician is higher than ServiceFlow's quote."
> - Lukas: "Let's compare total cost including dispatch time saved."
> - Carla: "I need a decision by end of January; our managing director signs above EUR 30K."
> - Agreed: technical session with Patrick on 24 Nov, 10:00.

**How the skill handles it**

- Step 1: a Demo transcript, so key quotes stay verbatim.
- Step 4: SSO is logged verbatim as deal-blocking, addressed; price is an open concern.
- Email: active evaluation, so semi-formal, plain text, 150–250 words, opening on a specific moment.
- Ground rule: the cost comparison has no date in the source, so none is invented.

## Example 2: Thin bullet notes from a check-in

**Request**

> Notes from today's Ardel Packaging check-in, recap + email please:
> - talked to Mia (ops)
> - renewal Q1, she's "not sure about the budget"
> - wants the export bug fixed
> - maybe add 10 seats?
> - send something next week

**How the skill handles it**

- Fragmentary bullets: recap what is there, note lost nuance, mark gaps.
- Ground rule 1: no date, surname or deadline is invented; the rep must specify "send something next week".
- The email stays short: the notes support no more.
- Ground rule 3: Mia's budget worry stays verbatim.

**Answer**

Built from short bullet notes, so some nuance may be missing.
